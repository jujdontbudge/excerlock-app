# Pillow audit: screenshots 088–175

## Scope and capture notes

This report covers **all 88 screenshots from `pillow-088.webp` through `pillow-175.webp`, inclusive**. The sequence contains four large systems:

1. alarm and preset configuration (`088–103`),
2. live sleep tracking and Sleep Aid playback (`104–134`),
3. Discover, personalization, tips, and challenges (`135–172`), and
4. Trends analytics (`173–175`).

The source files are 720×1681 pixels, but the bottom Mobbin attribution rail is not part of Pillow. The usable app capture is approximately 720×1560 pixels, consistent with a 390×844-point iPhone viewport scaled to about 1.846 px/pt. Measurements below are therefore inferred logical points, not raw screenshot pixels.

Pillow changes visual surface by task rather than forcing one background everywhere:

- configuration and analytics use a deep indigo-to-violet gradient;
- live sleep tracking uses an almost-black night surface;
- Discover feed and reading pages switch to a pale lavender-gray/white surface;
- immersive playback and completion screens use full-bleed photography with heavy color scrims;
- questionnaires use a calmer, nearly flat dark-violet field so selections carry the visual emphasis.

The sampled source colors in this range are approximately: content-gradient top `#1F0736`, content-gradient bottom `#7423A0`, questionnaire/sheet violet `#2E0D5A`, light Discover canvas `#EDE9F1`, and live-night background `#030003`–`#08020B`. These are observed screenshot colors and include compression/rendering variation.

## 1. Alarm-sound browser and preview states — `088–091`

### Purpose and hierarchy

This is a drill-in sheet for choosing an alarm sound. A centered **Alarm sound** title and back chevron sit under a small grabber. The first row, **iTunes or Apple Music / Choose song**, is separated from Pillow's own sound collection by a faint hairline. The rest is a plain vertical list: sound name on the left; stateful control on the right. There are no checkmarks or secondary confirmation buttons.

The selected item is communicated with a full-row translucent rounded highlight. Unavailable sounds show a cloud-download glyph; downloaded sounds show a circular play glyph; the actively previewing sound shows a pause glyph. That one trailing slot therefore explains availability, preview, and activity without adding labels.

### Index-by-index state ledger

- **`088`** — Initial chooser. **Old photos** is selected with the dark translucent row highlight and a play button. **Success** and **This is it** are also local/playable. **Fall**, **Opening Shop**, **Good Things To Come**, **Mallet Time**, **A Day For Smiles**, **Clouds**, and **Whale** show cloud-download icons.
- **`089`** — **Fall** has been tapped and its cloud changes to a spinner. **Old photos** remains selected, making download progress distinct from the committed choice.
- **`090`** — The download completes and **Fall** previews with a pause icon. **Old photos** still carries the selection highlight, so preview and selection are intentionally separate states.
- **`091`** — **Fall** becomes the selected row and retains the pause icon while playing; **Old photos** loses its highlight. This is the committed result.

### Copy and CTA rhythm

The page relies on direct manipulation rather than a bottom CTA: tap to download/preview/select, then use Back. Copy is extremely terse. The only explanatory line is the small **Choose song** subtitle under the external-library row.

### Motion and sheet implications

The inferred motion chain is cloud → spinner → play/pause, followed by a soft relocation of the row highlight. The sheet should slide in as a nested configuration layer, preserve scroll position, and return the chosen value immediately. Audio preview should stop or hand off cleanly on navigation.

### Color and layout

The sheet uses the standard dark-violet gradient, approximately 16 pt side insets, 52–56 pt rows, 14–16 pt selection radius, white primary text, and lavender secondary text. Controls remain white; selection is a darker translucent purple rather than the brand accent.

### ExcerLock mapping

Use this exact interaction grammar for **Settings → alert sound/haptic**, and for any condition-builder asset picker where items may need to load before preview. The useful lesson is the explicit separation of *downloaded*, *previewing*, and *selected*. ExcerLock should not apply this literally to locked-app selection, which needs visible multi-select state rather than audio-preview semantics.

## 2. Alarm-options hub and volume drill-in — `092–096`

### Purpose and hierarchy

The hub uses a short title bar followed by grouped translucent cards. The first group is a master **Alarm sound** switch and a selected-sound tile with a small square image, **Fall**, and **Main collection**. A second group contains **Alarm volume**, **iPhone vibrations**, **Alarm deactivation**, and **Snooze time**. Rows that navigate have chevrons and current-value subtitles; binary rows use native green iOS switches.

The volume drill-in has three levels:

1. a compact **Fade in** switch row,
2. an explanatory caption,
3. a larger control card with **Maximum volume**, a live percentage, slider, **Test** capsule, and AirPlay/output glyph.

An orange uppercase **NOTE** below the card explains that audio alarms use iPhone/iPad/external speakers but cannot use Apple Watch.

### Index-by-index state ledger

- **`092`** — Alarm-options hub with **Fall / Main collection**, **Volume: 80% – Fade in**, vibrations on, deactivation **Long press**, and snooze **Smart**.
- **`093`** — Volume detail at **80%**, with **Fade in** on. Supporting copy reads: **Increase the alarm volume gradually up to the maximum level below.** The control card provides **Test** and audio-output access.
- **`094`** — **Fade in** is switched off while maximum volume remains **80%**. The explanatory text stays visible, preventing the layout from jumping when state changes.
- **`095`** — Fade-in remains off and the slider moves to **100%**. The value label updates inline at the top-right of the control card.
- **`096`** — Return to the hub. **Alarm volume** now reads **Volume: 100%** and no longer appends **Fade in**; the rest of the hub is unchanged.

### Copy and CTA rhythm

The hub's subtitles act as confirmation, so there is no Save button. The volume page lets the user adjust, test, and leave. The orange note appears only after the main control and answers a likely hardware question without interrupting the action.

### Motion and sheet implications

Toggles and the slider update locally and instantly; the parent row summarizes the persisted result on return. **Test** is a small action capsule within the control card rather than a screen CTA. Slider movement should update the percentage continuously. The AirPlay icon is visually subordinate but stays inside the same functional card.

### Color and layout

Cards are roughly 8–10% white over the purple gradient, without borders or shadows. Green is reserved for native on/off state. Orange is reserved for caution/help copy and the output glyph, not for the main toggle. The slider track is thin and white with a large white thumb.

### ExcerLock mapping

This is a direct model for **Settings → notifications/general** and a strong model for **Manage → condition/preset options**. ExcerLock rows should similarly expose current values in subtitles, drill into one focused control at a time, and reflect the edited state immediately on return. A useful transfer is a test affordance for lock warnings or haptics, housed inside the setting card rather than promoted to a full-width CTA.

## 4. Preset editor, destructive confirmation, coaching, and notes — `100–103`

### Purpose and hierarchy

`100` is the most reusable Manage archetype in this range. It is an almost full-height editor sheet with layered top edges and a grabber. The hierarchy is:

1. a large editable name card, **Rise and shine**;
2. an **Alarm** master switch;
3. a white-selected **Time / Duration** segmented control;
4. a hero value card, **Wake up at 8.00 AM**, with a small pencil action;
5. rows for **Smart Wake-up** and **Alarm options** inside the same group;
6. independent toggle rows for **Audio recordings**, **Sleep aid**, and **Live Activity**;
7. a separated plain destructive row, **Delete preset**;
8. a centered white **Done** pill.

The **Smart Wake-up** row includes a green **ON** micro-badge plus a full explanatory sentence: **Alarm will go off at the lightest sleep stage between 7.45–8.00 AM.** Pillow allows one card to contain both a compact status and explanatory copy when the feature is unfamiliar.

### Index-by-index state ledger

- **`100`** — Fully populated preset editor. Alarm, audio recordings, and Live Activity are on; Sleep aid is off. The hero time is editable. **Done** is white, compact, and centered rather than full-width.
- **`101`** — Tapping **Delete preset** presents a native white confirmation alert: **Are you sure? / This action cannot be undone**, with **Cancel** in blue and **Delete** in red. The editor dims but remains spatially intact behind it.
- **`102`** — A pre-start instruction card on the purple gradient. A bordered **X Cancel** capsule sits at top-left. Two simple line illustrations explain device placement and unlocked/foreground behavior. Copy: **Place your device on the mattress, face-down, near your pillow.** Then: **You can lock your device but it’s best that you keep it unlocked with Pillow running in the foreground.** A centered white **OK** pill is followed by a divided **Always show** toggle row.
- **`103`** — **Notes** sheet, initially empty, with title and X, a **+ Add new…** row near the top, and a bottom-pinned **Sleep notes** switch. The content area stays empty instead of inventing placeholder copy.

### Copy and CTA rhythm

The editor's CTA sequence is *configure → optional destructive action → Done*. Procedure coaching uses *Cancel → visual instructions → OK → remember-this-choice*. Notes uses an action verb at the empty position (**Add new…**) and puts the global enable switch at the bottom.

### Motion and sheet implications

The preset editor and notes are draggable sheets. The delete confirmation is deliberately native and modal. `102` is an inset card rather than a loose tutorial page; the two illustrations should enter with the card, and **Always show** persists the preference. The layered sheet top in `100` suggests the originating page remains beneath it.

### Color and layout

The hero `8.00` uses a very light rounded face at roughly 60–70 pt, with a much smaller `AM`. Name and option cards use nested translucency; the selected segment is solid white with dark text. The `102` diagrams use orange for the device outline/attention bolt and white for the unlock glyph, while supporting text is deliberately low-contrast lavender.

### ExcerLock mapping

- `100` should drive the **Manage condition editor** and **weekly schedule/base allowance editor**: editable name, master enable, mode segment, hero value, advanced rows, local toggles, delete, Done.
- `101` is the direct delete-confirm pattern for a condition, locked-app group, or schedule.
- `102` maps to **Screen Time/Health/Location permission coaching**, **walk start positioning**, and **gym verification instructions**. It frames requirements as a successful setup procedure rather than a permission demand.
- `103` can become optional **workout/session notes** or a condition-description sheet.

## 5. Active tracking, notes, pause action, and paused state — `104–107`

### Purpose and hierarchy

The active tracking screen strips the UI to a black night surface. Two compact rounded rows at the top summarize the wake window (**7.45–8.00 AM**) and **Sleep Aid**, each with a leading glyph and trailing ellipsis menu. The center is dominated by the current time and small remaining-duration text. At the bottom are two equal circular controls: a stop square on the left and an eye/visibility control on the right.

### Index-by-index state ledger

- **`104`** — Active session at **11:27 PM**, **8hrs 33min left**. Stop and eye controls are neutral outlined circles.
- **`105`** — Notes sheet over the session. A selected note, **Trying to get a better sleep**, appears above **+ Add new…**; **Sleep notes** remains on in the bottom footer.
- **`106`** — Tapping the eye opens a minimal action layer while the session dims. The bottom choices are **Pause sleep tracking** with an orange eye glyph, then **Cancel**. The main time becomes gray, making the action layer the only active visual plane.
- **`107`** — Paused session at **11:28 PM**. Orange copy states **Sleep tracking paused**. The right eye button becomes a large filled orange resume action; stop remains a neutral outlined circle.

### Copy and CTA rhythm

Live screens avoid paragraphs. Status is a single line beneath the hero number. Secondary configuration remains available as compact top rows and ellipsis menus, while the bottom pair owns the session's primary actions.

### Motion and sheet implications

The eye interaction is a two-step safety pattern: tap → explicit **Pause sleep tracking** action → paused state. Pausing should not move the central clock or top rows; only contrast, status copy, and the right control change. Resume should be one tap on the now-prominent orange button. The notes sheet can be presented without stopping the session.

### Color and layout

The night background is nearly black (`#030003`–`#08020B`) with only a faint purple bloom. Top rows are neutral charcoal. Orange is withheld until the paused state needs attention. The clock is a 60–70 pt rounded-light numeral centered slightly above vertical midpoint.

### ExcerLock mapping

This is the best direct reference for **Walk session: active/paused**, and a strong shell for **Gym check-in while verification is running**. Translate top rows to condition/rate summaries, the hero to elapsed walk or earned minutes, and the two bottom circles to end/pause or details. Preserve the low-stimulation black surface; it signals that a real-world session is in progress.

### Color and layout

Alarm and failure screens use orange as the energy/attention accent over purple. Mood icons are large white disks with simple dark expressions. Failure recommendations are dark inset cards with orange bold headings and lavender body copy.

### ExcerLock mapping

- `108`/`111` map to **time running low/time out/blocked-app moments**: one soft recovery action, one deliberate hold-to-end or confirm action.
- `109`/`110`/`113` map to an optional post-exercise check-in and can also inspire gym verification outcome choices.
- `112` maps to a fixed-duration **walk session**.
- `114` is the strongest reference for **gym verification failed**, **walk ended unexpectedly**, or permission health failure. Explain the result and next steps in calm cards; do not scold.

## Deep recurring design principles in `088–175`

### 1. Pillow designs explicit state transitions, not isolated screens

The strongest sequences show state with minimal chrome:

- download → loading → preview → selected (`088–091`),
- switch/slider edit → summarized parent value (`092–096`),
- active → pause confirmation → paused (`104–107`),
- loading player → playing → collapsed mini-player (`120–122`),
- unordered → dragged → reordered → swipe-reveal → removed (`130–134`),
- questionnaire unanswered → selected → enabled next (`151–152`, `156–157`),
- available challenge → preflight → active → evidence (`165–171`),
- empty analytics → populated analytics (`173–175`).

ExcerLock should storyboard each feature as a similar state chain. A static good-looking page is not enough to reproduce Pillow's feel.

### 2. Surface color communicates mode

- **Purple gradient** = configuration, browsing, analytics.
- **Near-black** = a live session that should recede while the user sleeps/moves.
- **Light gray/white** = editorial reading and challenge comprehension.
- **Full-bleed image + scrim** = immersive playback or emotional completion.
- **Flat violet** = focused questionnaire where selected cards must dominate.

ExcerLock can preserve these role changes after the purple→orange reskin: warm dark gradient for normal app chrome, near-black for live lock/walk states, pale warm canvas for insights and challenge details, and full-bleed art only for emotional or immersive moments.

### 3. One level of emphasis per action

Pillow uses several CTA shapes with distinct roles:

- centered white pill for closing a sheet or confirming a screen (`100`, `102`),
- bright brand circle for next/back questionnaire navigation (`143–160`),
- full-width brand button for an explicit commitment (`167`, `168`),
- orange compact pill for time-sensitive alternate action (`108`),
- pink-red full-width button for destructive stop (`170`),
- text/arrow CTA inside editorial cards (`135`),
- whole-row tap for settings and selections (`097–098`).

Do not normalize these into one universal button. Their visual differences are part of the information architecture.

### 4. Parent screens summarize, children specialize

`092`, `096`, and `099` show the pattern clearly: a parent row contains label, current-value subtitle, and chevron; the child screen gives the control the full page. On return, the subtitle is the proof of persistence. ExcerLock Settings and Manage should follow this almost everywhere instead of placing every knob on one long page.

### 5. Settings use three kinds of switch intentionally

- Native green switches are used for device/session behaviors (`092`, `100`, `102`, `103`).
- Branded violet switches are used for content-preference categories (`140–141`).
- Selected option cards use full brand fills rather than switches (`147`, `152`, `155`, `157`).

For ExcerLock: keep platform/device permissions and behavioral booleans green; use orange brand switches for product-content preferences; use filled cards for mutually exclusive or capped choice questions.

### 6. Copy is progressive and consequence-aware

Pillow does not front-load every explanation. It puts:

- a one-line subtitle on the parent row,
- a concise explanation beside the actual control,
- hardware/edge-case guidance after the control,
- full FAQ only behind a secondary link,
- exact requirements immediately before challenge commitment,
- recovery recommendations only after failure.

ExcerLock should similarly sell the workout and benefit first, explain permissions when encountered, disclose condition rules before Save/Start, and reserve long troubleshooting for a failure sheet or FAQ.

### 7. Empty states preserve architecture

Favorites (`129`), challenge filters (`164`), and Trends (`173`) all retain titles, controls, tabs, and card shells. Only the content changes. This keeps users oriented and makes future data feel expected. ExcerLock's empty Analysis, no-condition Manage state, and no-locked-app state should never collapse into an unrelated blank page.

### 8. Motion is physical but structurally conservative

Pillow animates state inside stable geometry:

- highlight moves but rows stay sized;
- player controls swap without moving metadata;
- pause changes color/status but keeps the clock fixed;
- analytics bars grow inside unchanged cards;
- selections fill cards while bottom navigation stays pinned.

Use spring/haptic feedback for selection, reorder, pause/resume, and claim; avoid large layout jumps. Sheets use native slide/grabber behavior, wheel pickers use native physics, and external platform pickers remain fully native.

## Analysis, Settings, and Manage patterns to transfer

### Analysis

1. Use an **overview of independent metric cards**, each with one sentence, one micro-chart, one aggregate, and a chevron (`173–175`).
2. Keep the same cards for empty and populated states.
3. Use categorical color sparingly—icon and bars, not whole-card fills.
4. Separate personalized editorial insights from quantitative trends: Discover uses a light feed (`136–139`), Trends a dark dashboard (`173–175`). ExcerLock can keep both within Analysis as subroutes or segments.
5. Challenges are not merely badges; they need available, rule disclosure, active, progress evidence, stop, completion, and FAQ states (`164–172`).
6. Bookmarks, filters, and content preferences make a potentially long insight feed manageable (`136–142`).

### Settings

1. Use grouped parent rows with subtitles and chevrons (`092`, `096`, `099`).
2. Drill into one focused decision per page (`093–095`, `097–098`).
3. Put a brief explanation directly under unfamiliar controls and a note below the card for platform limitations.
4. Reflect saved values in parent subtitles on return.
5. Separate destructive actions spatially and confirm truly destructive actions with a native alert (`100–101`).
6. Use a dark sheet for contextual settings that belong to a tab/feed (`140–141`), rather than navigating away.

### Manage

1. The editor archetype is `100`: name, master enable, mode segment, hero value, advanced rows, local toggles, delete, Done.
2. Single-choice lists use whole-row selection and immediate persistence (`097–098`).
3. Long structured selections use descriptive cards and pinned navigation (`150–157`).
4. Collections support empty, populated, reorder, swipe-reveal, and removed states (`129–134`).
5. Exact rule summaries and dated evidence build trust (`168`, `171`). A condition should say what qualifies, then later show why a session did or did not qualify.
