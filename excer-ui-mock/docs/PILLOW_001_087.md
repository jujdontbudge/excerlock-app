# Pillow audit: screenshots 001–087

## Scope and reading notes

All 87 screenshots were visually inspected in indexed contact sheets, with individual originals opened for the major archetypes and state changes. OCR was used to verify small copy. The source files are 720 × 1681 px. The Pillow capture itself occupies approximately y=0–1559; the dark “Pillow / curated by Mobbin” strip at y=1560–1680 is external reference-site chrome and must not be reproduced.

This slice is not one uninterrupted navigation trace. Screens 001–009 are a sampler of major product surfaces, followed by a coherent onboarding and purchase flow at 010–036, diary/session management at 037–052, sleep/audio analysis at 053–077, and alarm-preset creation at 078–087. In particular, transitions such as 003→004 are not evidence that a paywall leads directly to an editor.

The ExcerLock mappings below are structural. Pillow’s sleep semantics should not be copied when they do not fit; its hierarchy, state handling, component grammar, and interaction rhythm should.

## I. Opening product sampler

### 001 — Launch / splash

- **Purpose and hierarchy:** A nearly empty launch frame: status bar, centered Pillow mark in the lower-middle, then the word “Pillow.” There is no explanatory copy or action.
- **Copy/CTA rhythm:** Brand only. The absence of a CTA makes this a transient state, not an onboarding page.
- **Motion/sheet implication:** The mark should softly resolve or fade into 010 rather than behave like a tappable screen.
- **Color/layout:** Full-bleed deep-indigo-to-purple gradient, with the small signature purple–magenta–orange pillow mark as the only high-chroma object. The large empty field makes the launch feel calm.
- **ExcerLock mapping:** Launch screen using the clay padlock/brand mark, with a short fade into onboarding or Home. Do not add slogans, legal copy, or progress indicators here.

### 002 — Completed personalization form

- **Purpose and hierarchy:** A large translucent circular identity chip sits above “Personalized Analysis,” a one-paragraph rationale, four rounded rows, then a centered white “Next” pill. Values are already complete: “Birth year 1995,” “Sex Female,” “Weight 116 lbs,” and “Height 5'4".”
- **Copy/CTA rhythm:** Benefit first, then the minimum requested data, then one decisive CTA. The form feels like confirmation rather than administration.
- **Motion/sheet implication:** Each row is a picker trigger; a completed value replaces the warm-orange “Select” label. The detailed picker sequence appears at 014–020.
- **Color/layout:** About 8% side margins for heading/form content; dark translucent rows on the gradient; white values; large circular glyph chip; white bottom action.
- **ExcerLock mapping:** Onboarding’s daily free-allowance and rate choices, or a compact “Your setup” confirmation. The same row grammar can support Manage → schedule/rate settings.

### 003 — Premium paywall, top/hero state

- **Purpose and hierarchy:** Full-width sleeping-person photography fades into purple. “Pillow Premium / Your free 7-day trial” leads into a three-step trial timeline: access today, reminder in five days, trial end in seven days. The pricing card begins at the bottom.
- **Copy/CTA rhythm:** Emotional image → clear offer → honest future events → price/action. “What’s included →” is orange and routes to details rather than bloating the hero.
- **Motion/sheet implication:** This is the top of a vertically scrollable paywall. The full purchase path is shown again at 030–036.
- **Color/layout:** Photography is darkened enough for white type; the brand gradient takes over below the image. Secondary timeline copy is lavender-gray, while the explanatory link gets the energy orange.
- **ExcerLock mapping:** Main paywall hero: active person or friendly padlock imagery, transparent trial timeline, and a separate “What’s included” route.

### 004 — Single-session range editor

- **Purpose and hierarchy:** A high sheet titled “Edit session” contains a Sleep/Duration segmented control, a categorical timeline, draggable range boundaries, From/To stepper rows, “Selected 2hrs 29min,” an Awake/Asleep choice, and paired “Done” / “Apply” actions.
- **Copy/CTA rhythm:** Visual manipulation first, exact values second, interpretation third, commit last. “Apply” is disabled until a meaningful change exists; “Done” remains available.
- **Motion/sheet implication:** The editor slides over a dim black context, keeps a drag handle, and supports direct manipulation plus minus/plus fine-tuning. Range handles and the selected overlay should track continuously.
- **Color/layout:** Near-full-height dark-purple sheet with round top corners. White selected segmented tab. Timeline categories use orange, pink, green, and blue. Bottom buttons distinguish navigation (purple “Done”) from state-dependent commit (pale disabled “Apply”).
- **ExcerLock mapping:** Manage → weekly schedule/off-days editor; condition-builder duration/rate setup; Analysis timeline scrubber. This is the strongest reference in the slice for editing a time window without leaving context.

### 005 — Session-detail overview

- **Motion/sheet implication:** A vertically scrolling detail page with a pinned or visually stable header/tab region and a persistent five-item tab bar.
- **Color/layout:** Data colors remain stable across chart and legend. Metrics sit in translucent rounded cards; hero numbers are much larger than labels.
- **ExcerLock mapping:** Analysis → day ledger: earned/drained timeline, reclaimed-time score, mood-equivalent “lock state,” note count, earned minutes, and screen-time minutes.

- **Color/layout:** Warm seasonal photography, rounded corners, dark caption scrims, tiny uppercase labels, and purple “NEW” chips over the persistent dark gradient.

## II. Onboarding: trust, personalization, permissions, completion

### 010 — Welcome

- **Purpose and hierarchy:** Pillow mark in a translucent aura, “Welcome,” one sentence—“Pillow is your smart sleep assistant to help you discover the life-changing benefits of good sleep”—and a bottom white “Next” pill.
- **Copy/CTA rhythm:** One benefit, one sentence, one action. The product sells the desired outcome rather than mechanisms.
- **Motion/sheet implication:** A page-to-page onboarding transition; the icon can enter with a restrained float/fade.
- **Color/layout:** Large empty top field, content clustered in the lower middle, white CTA above the home indicator.
- **ExcerLock mapping:** First onboarding sell screen: exercise earns guilt-free access; emphasize freedom/reward, not blocking.

### 011–013 — Privacy preface, detail, and return

- **Purpose and hierarchy:** 011 and 013 are the same consent page: shield glyph, “We Respect Your Privacy,” a concise on-device/iCloud promise, translucent “Learn more,” and white “I accept.” 012 is a white, nearly full-screen detail sheet/page with a purple headline, close button, and plain-language sections explaining anonymous use, local processing, iCloud, and Apple Health.
- **Copy/CTA rhythm:** Summary promise first; optional detail is available before consent; the primary acceptance action remains unambiguous. The flow returns to an unchanged consent page after closing detail.
- **Motion/sheet implication:** “Learn more” presents a scrollable light surface over the branded flow; close restores 013 with no lost state.
- **Color/layout:** The trust summary stays on the gradient. The long policy deliberately flips to black text on white for readability, with a purple close chip.
- **ExcerLock mapping:** Onboarding privacy promise before Screen Time/Health/Location requests; Settings → privacy detail. Explicitly explain that mock/demo state is local in this looks-only build and, in a real build, how activity/app usage would be handled.

### 014–020 — Progressive personalization with native-style pickers

- **Color/layout:** Four ~full-width dark pills stack with small gaps. Picker is a high white rounded rectangle with a faint gray selection band. Purple “Done” is the local commit; the screen-level action remains white.
- **ExcerLock mapping:** Pick daily free allowance, steps-to-minutes rate, workout target, or schedule times. For app selection, reuse the progressive row/commit rhythm but substitute an icon list rather than a wheel.

## III. Paywall and purchase state machine

- **034, native confirmation:** Continue presents the iOS App Store purchase sheet over a heavily dimmed paywall. Product, family subscription, one-week trial, annual price, renewal date, cancellation terms, account, and blue Subscribe are all native.

## Deep recurring design principles from 001–087

1. **Pillow makes one value enormous and everything else subordinate.** The Home ring, alarm time, preset duration, and recording total each become the page’s obvious answer. ExcerLock should do the same with spendable minutes, reclaimed time, or active-session progress.
2. **It preserves the shell across empty and populated states.** Compare 037/039, 055/077, and 078/079. The user learns one layout; data appears inside it. ExcerLock’s locked/unlocked and no-history/history states should not become unrelated compositions.
3. **Progressive disclosure is the default.** A toggle reveals dependent controls (080→081), a row opens one picker (014→015), a list item expands inline (063→064), and detail lives one push away (031–032, 087). The first surface stays understandable.
4. **Screen-level and local actions have different visual roles.** White pills (“Next,” “I accept,” “Done,” “Allow”) close or advance a whole screen. Purple pills commit an in-context brand action. Translucent pills are secondary. Orange signals attention, waiting, or physical urgency rather than generic primary action.
5. **Irreversible actions are staged.** Select first, show count, explain semantics if needed, then confirm using a native white alert or action sheet. Failure preserves the user’s work and offers Help.
6. **Charts are always paired with meaning.** A timeline is followed by hero stats; a stacked bar is followed by categorical cards; a filter is followed by an aggregate count and duration. ExcerLock charts should never be decorative or require the user to calculate the conclusion.
7. **Color is semantic and repeated.** Orange/pink/green/blue categories remain consistent between graph, legend, list, status, and mini-indicator. Green means enabled/startable/normal; red means destruction; orange carries attention or energy.
8. **The dark visual system is layered, not bordered.** Full-bleed gradients, 6–12% translucent cards, and slightly darker nested surfaces create depth. Borders and drop shadows are almost absent.
9. **Pillow freely mixes branded and native iOS surfaces.** Brand pages and sheets establish identity; OS permission prompts, App Store sheets, share sheets, action sheets, and alerts retain native trust. ExcerLock should not reskin system-critical moments into fake dialogs.
10. **Empty states explain causality.** 052 and 077 describe why nothing exists and how it will appear. They do not merely say “No data.”
11. **Friendly names turn configuration into objects.** “Rise and shine” makes a preset feel owned. ExcerLock conditions should have human names (“Morning walk,” “Gym day”) and previews, not only rule syntax.
12. **Motion is implied by affordance, not ornament.** Rings fill/pulse, carousels page, sheets spring, drag rows lift, wheel values coast, waveforms progress, and disabled/loading actions visibly change. Decorative animation should never compete with these state transitions.

## Onboarding, permission, and paywall pattern to transfer

The onboarding rhythm is: sell the outcome (010) → establish privacy (011–013) → collect only personalization that improves the result (014–021) → preframe each permission as a feature benefit (022–027) → acknowledge safety limits (028) → celebrate completion (029) → monetize with a transparent offer (030–036). It feels long because each page asks only one thing; it does not feel bureaucratic.

Permissions follow a clear decision rule:

- **Before every OS prompt:** use a branded page with a circular feature glyph, benefit-led headline, concise scope, and plain consequence.
- **Optional capability:** show Skip beside Allow/Continue, as in 022 and 026.
- **Foundational capability:** explain why it is required and omit Skip, as in 025; never threaten or moralize.
- **Operational limitation:** emphasize it inline in orange, as in 021 and 027.
- **Trust-sensitive detail:** provide a readable secondary document before consent, as in 011–013.
- **After denial:** this slice does not show a denial-recovery screen, so that behavior should be taken from later references rather than invented from 001–087.

The paywall is a state machine rather than one poster: emotionally resonant hero (003), explicit seven-day timeline, optional benefit detail (031–032), social proof and family value (033), repeated price/CTA, native store confirmation (034), disabled processing (035), and explicit success (036). Restore, terms, privacy, and cancellation reassurance remain visible. ExcerLock should preserve that transparency and should use live/localized store strings rather than drawing a price into an image.
