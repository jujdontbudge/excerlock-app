# Pillow forensic audit: screenshots 176–262

## Scope and reading notes

This report covers **all 87 screenshots from `pillow-176.webp` through `pillow-262.webp`, inclusive**. Every image was inspected visually in contact sheets and, where the copy or interaction state was dense, at native image resolution and with OCR as a cross-check.

The source images are 720 × 1681 px. The bottom approximately 120 px is the Mobbin curation footer, not Pillow UI; the usable app capture is therefore roughly 720 × 1561 px. Several pairs are not distinct routes but two scroll positions or before/after states of the same route. Those are called out so ExcerLock does not accidentally implement duplicate pages.

The audit uses the requested ExcerLock page names: **Onboarding, Paywall, Home, Condition builder, Walk session, Gym check-in, Manage, Analysis, Lock moments, and Settings**. When a Pillow screen has no honest one-to-one domain equivalent, it is labeled as a structural reference rather than forced into the product.

## Detailed flow audit

### 176–177 — weekly Trends digest; one long scroll

**Exact screenshots and states**

- **176:** middle of the weekly Trends digest. A partially visible week strip leads into three large stacked cards: Bedtime, Snoring, and Wake-up Mood.
- **177:** the same scroll continued downward. The bottom of Bedtime remains visible, followed by Snoring, Wake-up Mood, and a newly revealed Sleep Notes card.

**Purpose and hierarchy**

This is the summary-to-detail landing page for trends. Each topic is a self-contained, tappable mini-report rather than a generic navigation row. The card grammar is consistent: colored circular metric icon and bold title at upper left, circular translucent chevron at upper right, one-sentence interpretation, then a small bespoke visualization or hero value. The page gives enough meaning at a glance that drilling in is optional.

- Bedtime says, “Record sleep for at least 5 days each week to get more information about your bedtime trend.” Its chart uses orange vertical rounded ranges, dots, weekday initials, the date range `21–27 OCT 2024`, and right-edge times `10.30 PM` / `7.54 PM`.
- Snoring says, “This week’s average is 25min 51secs.” Three green rounded bars cross a dotted average baseline; `25m 51s` is enlarged at the right.
- Wake-up Mood says, “Your wake-up mood was mixed this week.” A large white circle with a purple question mark sits beside `Mixed` and the date range.
- Sleep Notes says, “One of the notes with the most impact on your sleep quality this week:” followed by the hot-pink pill `Trying to get a better sleep` and `-2%`.

**Copy and CTA rhythm**

The rhythm is **noun → plain-language interpretation → one memorable visual fact → chevron**. There is no global “See all” and no repeated button label. The whole card is the CTA, while the chevron makes that affordance legible. Empty or insufficient-data copy explains what action unlocks insight without sounding punitive.

**Motion/sheet implications**

Cards should press as one surface and push to their metric detail. Scrolling is continuous; 176 and 177 must be implemented as one screen. The mini-bars and dots are good candidates for a short staggered entrance when the week changes, but their calm read-at-a-glance role matters more than spectacle.

**Color/layout**

Cards sit nearly edge-to-edge with small gutters on the deep purple-to-violet gradient. They are darker translucent purple, borderless, softly rounded, and separated by roughly one small spacing unit. Metric colors stay categorical—orange Bedtime, green Snoring, yellow Mood, pink Notes—while titles stay white and interpretations use lavender-gray secondary text.

**ExcerLock mapping**

Use this almost directly for **Analysis**: `Time reclaimed`, `Earned by exercise`, `Blocked attempts`, `Best condition`, `Streak`, and an attribution card such as `Morning walk +12 min`. It also informs compact condition summaries on **Home**. The most important transfer is that every analysis card states the conclusion, not merely the metric name.

### 178–185 — Sleep quality analytic detail, empty/populated/range/compare states

**Exact screenshots and states**

- **178:** Week view for `20–26 Oct 2024`, empty chart, `Compared with None`, Best/Worst `-%`, and Average `-% / 0 sessions`.
- **179:** lower scroll position of that empty week. It reveals the distribution card: Good, OK, and Low are all `0` and `0% of total`.
- **180:** populated Week view for `21–27 Oct 2024`: three violet rounded bars; Best `69%` on 25 Oct, Worst `32%` on 23 Oct; Average `36%`, five sessions.
- **181:** lower scroll position of the populated week. The stacked average strip is mostly pink with an orange end segment; Good `0`, OK `1 / 20%`, Low `4 / 80%`.
- **182:** Month selected, `October 2024`; sparse short trend line near days 23–25; the same Best/Worst and `36%` average reaggregated over `1–31 OCT 2024`.
- **183:** Year selected, `2024`; one vertical October bar; the same summaries reaggregated over `1 JAN–31 DEC 2024`.
- **184:** `Compare with` modal sheet listing No comparison, Steps, Heart rate, Blood pressure, Cycling distance, Weight, Caffeine, Blood alcohol content, Dietary Calories, Temperature, and Snoring duration.
- **185:** populated Week view with `Snoring duration` selected for comparison. A green line and green right-side minute axis overlay the violet sleep-quality bars; the comparison row carries the green Snoring icon.

**Purpose and hierarchy**

This is Pillow’s strongest reusable analytic scaffold:

1. back + metric title;
2. Week/Month/Year segmented control;
3. previous/next period arrows and centered date label;
4. a deliberately sparse plot;
5. a full-width `Compared with` selector;
6. paired extrema card;
7. average/distribution card;
8. deeper content below the fold.

The scaffold is identical in empty and populated states. Empty data does not collapse the chart or remove summary cards; it replaces values with `-%`, `N/A`, zeros, and a quiet placeholder rail. This prevents layout jump and teaches the user what will eventually appear.

**Copy and CTA rhythm**

Controls use nouns and values, not prose. Summaries use short superlatives (`Best session`, `Worst session`, `Average`). The comparison selector is the single mid-page CTA and opens the sheet. In the sheet, the selected `No comparison` row is highlighted with a lighter translucent band; all rows use a large colored glyph and bold white label, with no confirmation button—selection should dismiss or update immediately.

**Motion/sheet implications**

- Week/Month/Year should preserve the scaffold and crossfade/replot the data, rather than navigate to separate pages.
- Period arrows should animate the date and marks laterally.
- The comparison list is a bottom sheet with a grabber, rounded top, dim/black backdrop, and enough height to feel like a focused mode.
- Selecting a comparison should dismiss the sheet and animate in a second axis/series; the original series retains its color.

**Color/layout**

The graph sits directly on the gradient, not inside a card. Faint horizontal rules and small violet axis labels recede behind thick rounded marks. Cards below use a darker translucent violet. Sleep-quality distribution uses green (Good), orange (OK), and pink (Low), while the core metric bars are violet. The white selected segment is the brightest chrome on the page.

**ExcerLock mapping**

This is the primary reference for **Analysis → Time reclaimed trend** and every secondary metric drill-in. Translate the comparison sheet to useful correlations such as Steps, Workout minutes, Active calories, Conditions completed, Unlock time used, or Blocked attempts. The empty/populated parity should also be used for fake demo states. The stacked distribution can become `On goal / Close / Below` or `Earned / Free allowance / Overage`, depending on the metric.

**ExcerLock mapping**

## Recurring design principles distilled from 176–262

### 1. A stable scaffold does more work than bespoke layouts

Quality, Duration, Disruptions, REM, Snoring, Mood, and Notes reuse a small number of shells. The product changes title, units, mark color, summary labels, and one metric-specific block—not navigation, spacing, or control placement. ExcerLock should build `MetricDetail`, `MetricChart`, `SummaryPair`, `DistributionCard`, `PeriodControl`, and `ComparisonRow` as shared primitives before composing Analysis screens.

### 2. Pillow states the conclusion before asking the user to inspect the chart

The Trends digest says “This week’s average is…” and “Your wake-up mood was mixed…” above each visualization. This is a major reason the product feels helpful rather than clinical. ExcerLock analysis cards should say `You reclaimed 2h 18m this week`, `Walking earned most of your time`, or `Your best day was Thursday`, then show evidence.

### 3. Empty states preserve information architecture

Empty charts keep axes, range controls, comparison rows, extrema cards, and category lists. They substitute placeholders (`-%`, `N/A`, zero) and one short explanation. This both avoids layout jump and previews future value. ExcerLock’s new-user Analysis should show the exact mature layout with calm placeholders and one path to earn the first data point.

### 4. Negative information is framed as diagnosis and recovery, not failure

Sleep disruption is orange, not red; diagnostics says `No data found`, names plausible causes, and provides Instructions. Cancellation ends with continued-access truth, not guilt. This tone matches ExcerLock’s “friend, not warden” mandate: lock moments should be factual, warm, and action-oriented.

### 5. Color is semantic and local

Within this range, quality is violet, duration is white, disruption/bedtime is orange, REM/notes is pink, snoring is green, and mood is white/yellow. Comparison data retains its own color. Controls remain white/violet; green is success/connected; orange also signals time-sensitive energy; system blue belongs to native UI. ExcerLock should assign colors to data families once and avoid recoloring a metric between cards and drill-ins.

### 6. Sheets establish spatial depth and trust boundaries

Settings, About permissions, Premium, diagnostics, Intercom, Siri, the time picker, and App Store cancellation all use sheets, but not the same sheet:

- branded sheets use the app gradient and rounded top;
- focused selectors use a grabber and compact list;
- native/system actions switch to white and platform-blue controls;
- prior sheets often peek behind the new one.

ExcerLock should preserve these layers. A branded fake OS dialog should never masquerade as native permission or subscription UI.

### 7. Immediate-commit settings do not need Save

Week start, theme, timeout, sensitivity, and toggles update instantly and reflect in the parent subtitle. Temporary precision controls such as time use a Done button inside a sheet. ExcerLock should reserve Save for multi-field condition creation, not simple choice lists.

### 8. Long explanations live after the value or behind an explicit help affordance

Metric education follows the summary below the fold; permission rationale lives behind `About permissions`; setting explanations precede only the choices that need them. This keeps the primary path calm while preserving transparency.

### 9. Rich assets are concentrated at moments of emotion or discovery

Most analytics use simple glyphs and geometry. Bespoke assets appear for alternate app icons, Siri/Pillow A.I., editorial photography, content cards, and the 3D win-back offer. ExcerLock should similarly reserve foundry assets for Home/condition heroes, onboarding/paywall, achievements, and condition-art selection—not decorate every settings row.

### 10. Scroll pairs reveal a deliberate above/below-fold rhythm

The top viewport establishes controls and visualization; the next scroll reveals interpretation, distribution, methodology, or education. ExcerLock Analysis should not cram every detail above the fold. Give the hero metric room, then reward scrolling with explanation and breakdowns.

## Pattern-specific guidance for ExcerLock

### Content/catalog pattern

Screens 234–235 use named rails, one-sentence collection framing, `VIEW ALL`, photographic cards, dark scrims, tiny categories, duration, NEW badges, and partial-card peeks. Apply this to a **condition catalog or exercise inspiration** only if product scope benefits from browsing. The **Condition builder artwork picker** should instead borrow 228’s coherent grid family and selected tile.

### Live-state pattern

This range does not contain a full active sleep-session screen, but 232 and 236 show the essential live/preset language: near-black environment, one large ring or one oversized value, a compact week/status strip, and a high-confidence action inside a tactile card. **Walk session** and **Gym check-in** should reduce chrome, use one dominant verification/progress object, and keep secondary stats in dark cards. A failed live state should borrow 259’s recovery hierarchy.

### Empty-state pattern

There are three useful variants:

1. **Scaffold-preserving analytic empty** (178–179, 186, 191, 194, 198, 201, 205): axes/cards remain, placeholder values appear.
2. **Centered dependency empty** (208): a single card explains what input is missing.
3. **Recoverable result empty/error** (259): cause + prominent fix + secondary back.

Choose based on whether the user is previewing future value, missing an upstream action, or needs repair.

### Share pattern

There is **no rendered share card or share sheet in screenshots 176–262**. The only direct product share entry is `Share Pillow` in Settings screenshot 214; screenshot 262’s browser toolbar also exposes the native share control. Do not claim this range defines Pillow’s share-card artwork. For ExcerLock’s achievement share card, the safest transfer from this range is the self-contained mini-report composition in 176–177/181 (title, plain-language result, one bold metric, one tiny chart, date range), exported onto a deliberately bounded surface and then handed to the native share sheet.

### Paywall/subscription pattern

Screens 247–252 establish a complete subscription-state machine:

`Free trial account truth → cancellation reason (gated) → optional win-back → native confirmation → Cancelled account truth`.

Key details are localized price, old-price strike-through, renewal fine print, explicit dates, a visible decline, system-owned final confirmation, and a post-cancel state that preserves access through the paid period. ExcerLock’s paywall should use the same clarity even in fake state.

### Permission and health pattern

Permissions have two layers: a glanceable health list (215–216) and a long benefit/data-use explainer (217–218). Diagnostics adds exact-scope selection and repair (257–259). Together these map almost perfectly to ExcerLock’s required permission onboarding and Settings health panel.

## Inferred layout and asset measurements

Measurements below are inferred from the 720 px-wide captures and should be validated in the target iPhone simulator rather than copied as raw pixels.

- **Capture/product bounds:** 720 × 1681 px total; app UI ends around y=1561 px and the ~120 px Mobbin footer must be ignored.
- **Logical scale assumption:** the capture behaves close to a ~2× export. Treat most pixel measurements as approximately half as many iOS points.
- **Primary content gutter:** about 28–45 px (roughly 14–22 pt) on sheets/forms; some dashboard cards use tighter ~14–18 px capture gutters. ExcerLock’s existing 16 pt token is the safe default.
- **Cards:** typically 90–96% of viewport width; corner radius visually about 28–40 px (14–20 pt); fill is dark/translucent with no visible border or shadow.
- **Grouped settings rows:** minimum touch height is visually around 80–100 px (40–50 pt), expanding for a subtitle. Section gaps are materially larger than row gaps.
- **Primary pills:** commonly 88–92% width on diagnostic/native sheets and about 82–100 px high (41–50 pt), with radius equal to half the height. Win-back CTAs are intentionally narrower, around half the screen, to remain centered beneath the offer.
- **Segmented controls:** nearly full content width, around 55–70 px high (28–35 pt); selected segment is solid white with dark text; track is a low-contrast translucent card.
- **Metric plot:** occupies roughly the upper 35–45% of the content viewport after the title/segments. Plots usually have only four to six faint horizontal rules and thick rounded bars, not dense gridlines.
- **Bottom tab area:** about 145–155 px including home-indicator safe area (~72–78 pt). Classic theme uses white; Black theme uses near-black.
- **Full-height branded sheet:** begins about 125–130 px below the top of the capture (~62–65 pt), with rounded top corners; underlying sheet/page may peek by 15–25 px.
- **Modal calendar card (257–258):** approximately 92% screen width; Check pill matches its width and sits immediately below with only a small gap.
- **Win-back hero asset (250):** spans roughly 90–95% of width and the top ~40% of the app viewport. It is a single coherent 3D composition, not scattered UI icons.
- **Alternate-icon assets (228):** three-column grid; individual app icons are roughly one sixth of screen width, while the selected backing tile grows to roughly one quarter width. Variants preserve material, silhouette, lighting, and padding.
- **Editorial/content assets (234–235):** cards use photo or rendered art with a dark bottom scrim, approximately 4:5 portrait aspect in horizontal rails, ~16 pt corners, and a visible next-card peek.
- **Reusable glyph assets:** metric chips are simple filled pictograms inside circles; permission/status glyphs are monochrome brand purple paired with separate green checks; chart marks are drawn geometry rather than raster art.
- **Palette sampled from captures:** common analytic backgrounds cluster around deep ink purples (`#1F083D`, `#290A4D`, `#3F1165`) and brighter lower violet (`#5C1E86`); Black theme clusters around `#000000`/`#1E1D28`; Premium uses dark plum around `#2A084A`. These are capture/compression approximations. ExcerLock should use its established orange token mapping rather than literal sampled Pillow values.
